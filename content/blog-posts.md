## What Is Attachment Style and Why Does It Matter?

Attachment theory was first developed by British psychologist John Bowlby in the 1950s, building on the work of Donald Winnicott and others. The core idea is simple: the way we were cared for as children shapes the way we seek connection as adults.

Your early experiences with caregivers create internal working models — mental blueprints that tell you things like:

- Whether people are trustworthy
- Whether you're worthy of love
- Whether closeness is safe or dangerous

These blueprints operate below conscious awareness. You don't choose them. But they influence every relationship you have, from your romantic partner to your closest friends.

## The Four Attachment Styles

Research has identified four primary attachment styles:

### 1. Secure Attachment Style

People with a secure attachment style are comfortable with intimacy and independence. They can lean into closeness without losing themselves, and they can handle separation without excessive anxiety. They tend to have healthy, stable relationships built on mutual trust and open communication.

### 2. Anxious Attachment Style

Those with an anxious attachment style crave closeness but live in fear of abandonment. They tend to interpret ambiguous situations as signs their partner is pulling away. They may become clingy, seek constant reassurance, or experience intense emotional highs and lows.

### 3. Avoidant Attachment Style

People with an avoidant attachment style value independence above almost everything else. They may seem emotionally distant, struggle with vulnerability, and create distance when relationships get too close. They often convince themselves they don't need anyone.

### 4. Disorganized Attachment Style

Sometimes called fearful-avoidant, this style involves a mix of anxious and avoidant traits. People with disorganized attachment often want closeness but simultaneously fear it. This style is often linked to childhood trauma or inconsistent caregiving.
